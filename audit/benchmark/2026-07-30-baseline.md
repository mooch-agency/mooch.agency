# Baseline benchmark: pipeline vs Notion audit skill

**Run date:** 30 Jul 2026
**Pipeline commit:** b33a613 (master), prompt UNCHANGED
**Purpose:** one baseline, before any prompt pruning, so the next run can be attributed.

Do not edit the judge prompt until this table is filled in. That is the whole point
of the run: a prune measured against nothing tells you nothing.

## Method

**Sequential, not parallel. The pipeline must run first.**

1. Pipeline, locally on the subscription engine ($0 marginal), picker running free.
2. Read `picker.pages_used` out of each record.
3. Notion audit skill on the same site, in a **fresh chat per site**, pinned to
   those exact URLs.
4. Record both, then verify.

### Why the pages are pinned, and what it costs

Both tools reading 5 pages is not enough: if each picks its own 5, the comparison
is still page selection, not judgement. Holding the evidence identical turns this
into a clean A/B on the judge, which is what the ticket is actually asking.

The tradeoff is explicit: **this run does not test the picker.** Page selection is
a real part of the pipeline's performance and it is deliberately excluded here.
Assess it separately by noting which pages the Notion skill would have chosen on
its own (its priority list is in its output) and whether any of them look richer
than what the picker took.

Do not cap the skill by editing its Notion page. That skill is the live
cold-outreach tool, where 12-18 pages is the point. Cap it in the prompt.

**Verification shortcut: only verify where the two tools disagree.**
Where both flagged the same thing, two independent instruments agree and it is
almost certainly real. Where both missed something, this run cannot tell you.
The disagreements carry all the information, and they are maybe 40% of the rows.
This is the method register's own rule: never verify with the auditor's own
instrument.

### The coverage confound: record pages read, always

The Notion skill targets **12-18 pages loaded in slow mode**, chosen from a 15-20
page priority list built via `web.search`. The pipeline reads **5**. A raw
finding count therefore compares coverage, not judgement.

Split every miss into two buckets or the numbers mean nothing:

- **Out of scope** — the other tool found it on a page we never read. That is the
  5-page product decision working as designed, not a defect.
- **In scope** — the other tool found it on a page that WAS in our bundle. That is
  a real judgement miss. On apexvolumetrics this was 6 of 7.

Only the second bucket is evidence about the judge.

### Prompt asymmetry worth noting

Two instruction-level differences between the tools, both relevant to the
recall gap:

- The Notion skill says *"Be confident on things you can verify... Don't hedge."*
  The judge prompt has no equivalent counterweight anywhere: six or seven FP
  guards all push one way.
- *"Don't flag intentional design choices"* appears in BOTH prompts, so that
  guard is not what separates them.

### Notion prompt template

One per site, **fresh chat each time**, with `<URLS>` replaced by that site's
`pages_used`. Same wording for all five.

```
Run a content audit on <DOMAIN>, but audit ONLY these pages, and do not load any others:

<URLS>

Skip the web.search discovery step entirely: the page list is fixed above. Everything else about how you run the audit is unchanged.

Then, separately from the report, tell me: which of those URLs you managed to load, which you couldn't read, and which pages you WOULD have picked if the list hadn't been fixed.
```

The last question is what keeps the picker in the picture: it captures the skill's
own page priorities without letting them affect this run's findings.

## Results

### 0. Read this before the tables: the coverage confound ran backwards

The method anticipated the Notion skill reading **more** pages than the pipeline and
warned that a raw finding count would therefore flatter it. On this run the opposite
happened, and it is the single most important result here.

The pages were pinned, so page *selection* was held equal as designed. What was not
equal was page *rendering*. On three of five sites the Notion skill failed to render a
pinned page that the pipeline read in full:

| Site | Page | Notion | Pipeline | Ground truth (verified independently) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lisbon.amplify.eu | `/buy-series.html` | "no content at all" | read the full pricing table | **Populated.** 9 packs, prices, per-session rates |
| r3p.xyz | `/insights/` | "title only, no body" | read it, flagged title drift | **Populated.** Article index, 3+ posts with dates |
| propstrata.com | `/support` | "failed in both modes" | read it | Empty over plain HTTP; pipeline saw rendered content |

In two of those three, Notion did not merely miss findings, it **manufactured a
critical/high finding out of its own fetch failure**: "Buy series page is empty"
(critical) and "Insights page is empty" (high). Both are false. Verified in a JS
renderer: buy-series shows `39.00 €`, `22.00€`, `99.00 €` … through `435.00 €`;
`/insights/` lists "The Invisible Force That Built the World" (Jun 15, 2026) and
others.

Two consequences for how the rest of this file reads:

1. **Notion's headline counts are inflated and the pipeline's are not directly
   comparable to them.** 2 of Notion's 4 critical/high findings across the set are
   artefacts of its own renderer.
2. **The pipeline's fetching is a genuine strength, and it is currently the only
   part of this comparison that clearly wins.** Do not "fix" the pipeline toward the
   Notion skill's behaviour on the basis of raw counts.

This does not rescue the judge. Where both tools held the same rendered text, the
recall gap is real and large. That is what the tables below isolate.

### What the scoreboard actually says

**The recall gap is real and it is not a coverage artefact.** On apexvolumetrics,
the one site where both tools read byte-identical evidence, the pipeline found 1 and
missed 6 verified-real findings that were sitting in its own bundle, including a dead
primary CTA and a FAQ page that is three-quarters empty. No page-count argument
touches that number.

**But the pipeline's precision is genuinely better, and that is not nothing.**
Verified FPs: pipeline 0, Notion 4. Two of Notion's four were critical/high findings
invented from its own render failures; the other two (apex nav drift, apex footer
Pricing) are cases where the pipeline explicitly considered the same evidence and
correctly declined. The near-zero-FP rule is doing real work. The problem is that it
is the *only* thing doing work.

**The two tools fail differently, which is the useful part.** The pipeline misses
things it never looked at and things it explained away. Notion misses nothing it can
see, and hallucinates about things it cannot. A pipeline that fetched like the
pipeline and judged like the Notion skill would have found essentially everything real
in this benchmark.

**The floor and the gate both cost real findings tonight.** The quote gate dropped 2
findings, both verified real (lisbon FAQ/Barre title, r3p Insights title drift). Four
of five sites shipped as "thin" despite carrying verified-real issues, including
apexvolumetrics, whose hero CTA points at an NXDOMAIN.

### First: the raw log is empty, and it is not a bug we can fix

The file opens with the pipeline's own error string, which blames
`display:"summarized"` on the judge call. **That diagnosis is wrong, and it cost a
cycle.** `display` was already set correctly.

The real cause: this benchmark ran on `AUDIT_LLM=subscription`, which shells out to
`claude -p`. That path has no thinking parameter, and `llmCall` hardcodes
`thinking: ''` for it (`run-pipeline.mjs:66`). Verified three ways on
claude-opus-4-8, 30 Jul:

- `--output-format json` → envelope has no thinking field at all
- `--output-format stream-json --verbose` → zero thinking content blocks
- `+ --include-partial-messages --effort high` → zero `thinking_delta` events

**A subscription run cannot produce a raw reasoning log at any setting.** This is an
engine limit, not a config error. Getting one means running that site on
`AUDIT_LLM=api` and paying for it.

Corrected in code on 30 Jul: the comment at the judge call now says `display` is
API-path-only, and the empty-log message now names the engine-specific cause instead
of sending the next reader back to `display`.

**Correction to an earlier claim in this file's work log:** I first wrote that this
blocks step 2. It does not. Step 2's test is whether the *output* moves when the
Section A duplicates are cut, and outputs are fully captured on the subscription
path: findings, severities, `approach`, `rejected`. Thinking would tell us *why* a
result moved, not *whether* it did. Step 2 is measurable now. What is genuinely lost
is diagnostic depth if step 2 produces a surprise, in which case re-run the surprising
site on the API path.

Everything in the section below is therefore inferred from `approach`, `rejected` and
the judge's final message, not from reasoning we can see.

### What it does answer

The ticket asks whether each miss was considered and rejected, or never looked at.
Even without the chain of thought, the six apex misses split three ways, and the
split matters more than the count:

**A. Looked, checked, and reached the wrong conclusion (3).** The judge's closing
line is:

> "The rest checks out: $49/month, $50K threshold, 'top 1%', and 10,000+ addresses
> are all consistent across pages."

Every one of those three is a miss. It named the exact fields, asserted consistency,
and was wrong on the threshold ($50K "Default" vs "hard-coded") and on the counts
("10,000+ Addresses" vs "thousands of expert trader addresses", same page). This is
the most alarming bucket: **it is not under-looking, it is confidently mis-concluding,
and it wrote the false conclusion down as a positive claim.**

**B. Looked, and explained the evidence away (1).** On the empty FAQ:

> "FAQ and Data Integrity pages are thin (accordions collapsed), limiting cross-checks
> on FAQ answer text."

It noticed the missing text, invented a mechanism that would make it benign, and
moved on. The mechanism is false: there is no answer text in the served HTML at all.
This is FP-suppression working exactly as instructed, on a critical finding. The same
pattern recurs on propstrata `/blog` ("almost entirely footer boilerplate") and, from
Notion's side, on samble `/knowledge-continuity`. **Three instances in one benchmark
of "page looks empty → assume rendering, not defect."**

**C. Never looked (2).** The outcome-promises contradiction and the three-names-for-
one-page issue appear nowhere: not in `findings`, not in `rejected`, not in
`approach`. Genuine recall failure.

Plus one structural, outside all three: the **dead hero CTA**. The judge could not
have found it at any prompt setting, because `bundle` carries url/title/text and never
`p.links`. That is Done-when item 4, not a judgement problem.

### The finding that should change the plan

`rejected` had **5 entries and not one of them was a miss.** All five were correct
declines, and one (nav drift) was a Notion FP the pipeline was right to refuse. Every
real miss lived in `approach` prose, or nowhere at all.

So the skip corpus in Done-when item 5, which is built from `judge_log.rejected`,
**would have surfaced zero of the six real misses on this site.** It is measuring the
declines the judge was confident enough to enumerate, which is precisely the set least
likely to contain its errors. Raising the #58 cap from 6 does not fix this; the misses
were never candidates for the list.

That is a direct argument for the confidence layer (step 3) over more corpus work, and
an argument that bucket A ("asserted consistent, was wrong") needs its own treatment:
a confidence field does not help a judge that is *certain* and *wrong*.

### Why the prune was reverted rather than kept

Keeping it would put an unmeasured change into the live client-facing judge, which is
the exact thing this file was written to prevent. The prompt is back to the 552-word
baseline, verbatim, verified by diff. Nothing about the judge has changed tonight.

### What this means for steps 2 and 3

Both are currently unrunnable as written, for the same reason. Before either:

1. **Quantify the noise floor.** Run one site n=5 on an unchanged prompt and record
   how many findings are stable. Tonight's n=4 on lisbon suggests roughly 1 stable,
   2-3 volatile, but that is one site.
2. **Change the unit of measurement from "the finding set" to "the stable finding
   set."** Compare findings that recur across n runs, not a single run's output. That
   costs n× the runs, and at $0 marginal on subscription the only real cost is
   wall-clock.
3. **Only then prune,** and judge the result against the noise floor rather than
   against a single baseline run.

This also puts a caveat on the benchmark above: **the low-severity rows in the results
tables are one sample of a noisy distribution.** The high-value verified findings
(apex FAQ, $50K, dead CTA, Nvidia CTO) are unaffected as *facts about the sites*, but
"the pipeline found this and Notion did not" at `low` severity should be read as one
draw, not a property of either tool.

### A cheaper read on the same evidence

Every one of the volatile findings is `low`. The one 4/4 stable finding is `medium`.
That is consistent with the confidence-layer hypothesis in step 3 (section E of the
prompt audit): the judge already behaves as if it has a confidence axis, it just has
nowhere to put it, so marginal calls surface or vanish at random. Worth noting that
step 3 might be reachable **before** a clean step 2, since a confidence field is
partly a fix for the very noise that makes step 2 hard to measure.
